About these records

Where the names come from, and what they do not say

Official records

The list is built straight from the archived PDFs by scripts/build-bio-reference.py, so anyone can rebuild it and check it against the page numbers. It covers the Tiger Conservation Plan 2025-26 to 2035-36 (Rajasthan Forest Department, mainly Annexures 5 and 6), the Preliminary Bird Guide of Mukandra Hills Tiger Reserve and Amphibians & Reptiles of Mukandra Hills Tiger Reserve (both Wildlife Institute of India and Rajasthan Forest Department, 2025), and four plants named in the 2020 eco-sensitive zone notification. A few species named only in the plan's chapter on flora and fauna, such as dhok (Anogeissus pendula), are added with those pages.

Status letters are the plan's own: A abundant, C common, L less common, R rare, I introduced. They are not a population estimate. The WII guides give a local status from their own surveys, the IUCN category and the schedule under the Wild Life (Protection) Act as amended in 2022.

They add {{ inatBiodiversity.summary.countsByGroup.Insects }} insects, {{ inatBiodiversity.summary.countsByGroup.Arachnids }} arachnids and {{ inatBiodiversity.summary.countsByGroup.Fungi }} fungi, groups the official documents do not list.

The project's place, “MHTR Kota”, takes in the reserve and the surrounding countryside and town, so a community record does not by itself show that a species occurs inside the notified boundary. Research Grade is iNaturalist's own quality label, not a check by us, and counts are records, not abundance. Download the snapshot (JSON, CC BY 4.0).

Photos, descriptions and Red List categories

Each card is matched to its taxon on iNaturalist{% if bioTaxaClient.fetchedAt %} (checked {{ bioTaxaClient.fetchedAt }}){% endif %}. From there we take the global IUCN Red List category, any status recorded for India, whether the species is native or introduced in India, and the opening lines of the Wikipedia article (CC BY-SA 4.0, linked on the card). Photos are iNaturalist's curated reference photos and appear only when the photographer has licensed them CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA, with their name and the licence under the picture. They identify the species; they are not photographs from the reserve.

Nothing observation-level is stored or shown: no coordinates, dates, observer names or observation IDs. The “records in the MHTR project” link simply opens that species inside the public project.

Names

Plain spelling slips in the printed lists are corrected and the printed form is kept in the notes. Where the plan and a WII guide use different names for the same bird or reptile, the row follows the WII guide and keeps the plan's name as printed. A few printed names cannot stand as they are, for example Canis pardus for the wolf or Gazella gazella for the chinkara; those rows say so instead of being changed quietly. Bird families follow the IOC World Bird List v15.2 (Gill, Donsker & Rasmussen, CC BY 3.0). Where the plan prints no family, the card shows the family from iNaturalist and says so.

The WII bird guide describes 219 species that visitors are likely to see; it is not a full checklist, so birds printed only in the plan are kept with the plan as their source. The plan's aquatic and fish lists include species such as the Ganges river dolphin, hilsa and golden mahseer; they are listed because the plan lists them, and the plan gives no date or place for them.
